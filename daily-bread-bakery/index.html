Little Manila · North York

Filipino bread on Wilson Avenue

Daily Bread Bakery is a small Filipino bakery at 332 Wilson Ave, in Toronto’s Little Manila. The shop’s own sign says it is specialized in Filipino bread, hot pandesal (sweet buns), ensaymada, pandecoco, monay, Spanish bread and more.

From the bakery’s own sign

Pandesal

The sign calls them “hot pandesal (sweet buns).” Reviewers describe them as soft and fluffy, and say they stay soft rather than going hard.

Ensaymada

Listed on the shop sign. Reviewers come back for it; one visitor on Heritage Toronto’s Little Manila walking tour returned after the tour to buy ensaymada.

Pandecoco, monay and Spanish bread

All three are named on the yellow sign over the door, along with “Filipino bread” and “and more.” Mabuhay Canada (October 2025) also wrote about the bakery’s Spanish bread and a newer ube marble loaf recommended by the owner.

The breads named above are taken from the bakery’s own storefront sign, photographed on the Google listing, and from reviews and a Mabuhay Canada article dated October 19, 2025. The bakery does not publish a full menu or prices online, and none are listed here.

A Little Manila bakery since 2003

Kislap and The Grind magazine both describe Daily Bread Bakery as a Filipino bakery on Wilson Avenue run by Lorna “Judy” Leal since 2003. Mabuhay Canada, writing about a walking tour on October 18, 2025, also names Judy Leal as the owner and calls the bakery a Little Manila staple.

Heritage Toronto has included the bakery on its “Meals and Migration in Little Manila” walking tour (a Heritage Toronto photo from May 22, 2017 shows tour participants in front of the shop). A visitor in a Google review this year wrote that Heritage Toronto’s walking tour of Little Manila brought them here.

Toronto Public Health last inspected the bakery on October 23, 2026 and recorded a pass, with no infractions that day (DineSafe record for 332 Wilson Ave).

Party and bulk orders

The bakery does not publish a party-order menu or a list of trays online. Reviewers have written about picking up breads and ensaymada for gatherings — including a five-year-old Google review about desserts brought to an office party — but those are customer accounts, not a formal catering offer.

If you are planning a party or a larger order, the surest route is to call and ask.
